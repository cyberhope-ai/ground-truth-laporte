VIGO COUNTY COUNCIL
Special Call Meeting Minutes
Tuesday September 2, 2025 at 3:30 P.M.
Council Chambers, Vigo County Government Center

Pledge of Allegiance

President David Thompson called the meeting to order at 3:35 p.m.
Calling of the Roll

Randy Gentry — present, Nancy Allsup — present, Brenda Wilson — present, Steve Ellis, -
present, Vicki Weger — present, Brad Anderson ~ present, David Thompson — present.
Council President David Thompson explained this special call meeting of the Council was for
the Vigo County School Corporation and other elected officials to provide information on future
plans for the School Corporation and the school facilities in the County. David said this was
purely an information gathering meeting, and there were would be no public comment. He said if
a decision from Council was necessary on any issue related to School Corporation plans, the
decisions would be made in a future public Council meeting with public comments.
Vigo County Commissioner Mark Clinkenbeard gave an overview of local schools and the need
for upgrades to the facilities and an action plan. He noted SB 382 was new legislation
empowering Indiana counties to transfer or gift funds to school corporations.
Kristen Craig with the Terre Haute Chamber of Commerce took the podium and went over
business opportunities, new talent, and investments in the area she says were lost due to the
status of local schools.
Vigo County School Corporation Superintendent Chris Himsel gave a summary of Vigo County
School Corporation’s proposed reorganization plan.
He outlined a drop in forecasted enrollment. Himsel said in the 2029-2030 school year, Vigo
County Schools are forecasted to have less than 12,500 students. He said the forecasted amount
of students will need half of the space Vigo County is currently maintaining and outlined
potential reasons and causes for the enrollment drop.
Himsel said the amount of money needed to preventatively maintain Vigo County schools was
close to $23 million per year. He said due to how various laws allow tax revenue to be
distributed to schools in Indiana, $9 million is available per year to preventatively maintain the
schools. He said the underfunding of preventative maintenance has the potential to create a
snowballing impact on the status of area schools.
Himsel then went over the additional needs of the school corporation. This list included
expanding the number of full-day Pre-K, additional Alternative School opportunities, and
consolidating programs to prevent cuts to student opportunities.

Himsel said the proposed plan tackles the need to reduce the amount of buildings to maintain.
This plan would reduce the elementary schools to 10, 4 middle schools, and two high schools.
He said North and South high schools would be renovated into Middle Schools. West Vigo
would be renovated and a new high school would be built east of the Wabash River. Himsel
noted Deming Early Learning Center, Dixie Bee, Sugar Grove, and Farrington Grove would
become early learning/alternative learning center. He briefly discussed the logistics of where
students would attend during the construction process. He said the construction process would
take 2-3 years if started in 2026.

Himsel said the corporation’s ask from the Council would be to come up with public/private
partnerships to help fund parts of the proposed project. He outlined three “groups” of the project
which need completed. Group 1: Construct a new high school east of the Wabash River. Group
2: Modernize West Vigo High School, DeVaney Elementary, Fayette Elementary, Franklin
Elementary. Group 3: Renovate and modernize North, South, and Consolidated into Middle
Schools alongside modernizing Woodrow Wilson Middle School.

Chris Switzer took the podium and explained Senate Bill 382 again. He said the legislation
authorized the creation of an oversight board to monitor the use of funds. The board will monitor
the use of funds and will have appointees from various government bodies. He said the
Commissioners will come before the Council to ask for $100,000 to be appropriated to help
establish and fund the oversight Board.

The floor was then opened to questions.

Brenda Wilson asked what the size of the potential new High School would be. Himsel said the
new high school would be large enough to teach 2600-2700 students. He said the career center
will be utilized to manage space.

Steve Ellis asked why the Corporation was not renovating the old schools instead of building
new ones while moving all students to one of the schools. Himsel said the Corporation
considered this option, but was unsure if the infrastructure of either of the high schools could
handle the amount of students even for a short time. Himsel discussed various issues he said
were related to not building a new School or Career Center. He said the plan would bring Terre
Haute Schools more in line with the rest of the state. Steve asked how many properties were
potentially going to be closed. Himsel said at least four. Ellis expressed concerns with the
potential cost of the project and creating a situation where parents remove children from the
corporation due to seeking a smaller school experience. Himsel noted the concerns and reiterated
this project would create less buildings to maintain and allow the corporation to live within their
means more effectively due to the limited ways schools can capture revenue in Indiana.

Randy Gentry asked about the potential cost of the project. Himsel said there were a large
amount of variables which would impact cost, one being when the construction started. Himsel
said the School Corporation’s current funding capacity was about a $10.5-11 million levy spread
out over 20 years to pay back. He said in hard construction costs that was about $150 million.
Randy asked how much the county would need to contribute. Himsel said it would depend on the
Council’s willingness to cooperate with the plan. Randy also expressed his trepidation with the

creation of an oversight board. Himsel said the oversight board would allow for partnership.
Chris Switzer said the oversight board would recommend appropriations to the Council to make
related to this project. Randy asked about amount of properties vacated after the plan. Himsel
said a minimum of four and potentially up to seven. Randy asked about the potential of the
Corporation leveraging the value of the vacated properties to help fund the proposed plan.
Vicki Weger and Brad Anderson expressed thanks to Himsel for appearing before Council and
answering questions.
Steve Ellis reiterated his concerns about the proposed plan.
The meeting was adjourned at 4:47PM.
