Communications from Elected Officials or Agencies of the County
Commissioner Chris Switzer thanked the Council Annual Budget Committee for their work over
the past several weeks.
Jon Shackleford with the Honey Creek Fire Protection District approached the Council to give an
update on their operations. He said after the 2023 tornado outbreak the district established the
structure collapse entrance rescue teams. He provided an overview of the teams’ operations. Jon
said an assessment after the 2023 outbreak showed the district did not have the training,
equipment, or funding to properly respond to similar disasters. Jon noted the shortage of
response teams in natural disasters of this nature. He said there would be a second class for 19
firefighters later this month. Jon thanked the Council for making this operation possible through
approval of the district’s budget.
Judge Lakshmi Reddy approached the Council to express further support for the Family Court
project. She said she was informed the project would not be fully funded being part of cuts the
Annual Budget Committee made. Judge Reddy noted continuing problems with population of
local jails and the expenses related. She said her family court project is early intervention and she

believed this would make a difference. Judge Reddy once again requested the Council revisit this
project and encouraged fully funding it.
Vigo County Assessor Kevin Gardner approached the Council to explain a situation around
Saturn Petcare not complying with the stipulations of their tax abatement. He said in 2024 Saturn
Petcare did not file a personal property return. He said Saturn Petcare received a tax bill due to
the lack of response from Saturn. Kevin said Saturn did not pay the bill, so they were marked as
delinquent. Kevin said in 2025, Saturn did file a personal property return without information.
Kevin then outlined the County’s various communications with Saturn. He said since Saturn was
not in compliance that one year, this could lead to their abatement being removed. David
Thompson asked about staffing changes at Saturn. Kevin said he was not sure about details of
Saturn’s staffing. There was discussion among the Council about whether Saturn Petcare’s non-
compliance for in this situation warranted action from the Council. Kevin Gardner said it was his
personal belief this situation didn’t warrant a removal of the abatement. Michael Wright Saturn
Petcare was in compliance said outside of this situation. He said he would try to get something
into writing the Council was comfortable with dictating what was expected of Saturn Petcare in
relation to fees and/or action as a result of this situation sometime in the future.

a. First Reading of the Vigo County 2026 Budget
Auditor Larry Hutchings noted the first reading of the Vigo County Solid Waste Management
District and the Terre Haute International Airport’s annual budget would need to happen at a
later date due to inadequate time between him filing a notice to taxpayers and this public hearing.
He said these two binding units’ budgets would be publically read at a special call meeting
September 18" at 3:30pm.
Auditor Larry Hutchings gave the first reading of the 2026 annual budget for Vigo County as
follows: Notice to taxpayers is available online at www.budgetnotices.in.gov or by calling 888-
739-9826. Complete details of budget estimates by fund and/or department may be seen by
visiting the office of this unit of government at 131 Oak St. Terre Haute, IN 47807. Notice is
hereby given to taxpayers of Vigo County, Indiana that the proper officers of Vigo County will
conduct a public hearing on the year 2026 budget. Following this meeting, any ten or more
taxpayers may object to a budget, tax rate, or tax levy by filing an objection petition with the
proper officers of Vigo County not more than seven days after the hearing. The objection petition
must identify the provisions of the budget, tax rate, or tax levy to which taxpayers object. Ifa
petition is filed, Vigo County shall adopt with the budget a finding concerning the objections in
the petition and testimony presented. Following the aforementioned hearing, the proper officers
of Vigo County will meet to adopt the following budget. Public hearing date — Tuesday,

c. Resolution 2025-2: Resolution Modifying Local Income Tax Rates Vigo County
Auditor Larry Hutchings said this resolution concerned the fire departments in the County and
the amounts that will be coming in based on the calculations used. These distributions were
based on a 65/35 split consistent with years past. The Council voted on this split in the August
meetings. This resolution was discussed at the sunshine meeting. Randy Gentry asked when the
deadline to approve these LIT rates and distributions to local fire departments. Michael Wright
said October 31°. Chris Switzer gave an overview of various LIT rates and how the funds
containing the money captured by the LIT can be used. Terre Haute mayor Brandon Sakbun
approached the Council and how the LIT process would change in 2027 due to new state laws.
Randy Gentry made a motion to approve Resolution 2025-2 as presented. Brenda Wilson
seconded the motion. Upon a roll call vote for the pending motion, voting was as follows: Randy
Gentry — aye; Nancy Allsup — aye; Brenda Wilson — aye; Steve Ellis — aye; Vicki Weger — aye;
Brad Anderson — aye; David Thompson — aye. With a vote of 7-0, the motion passed.

b. Additional Appropriation 2025-48: CASY — Substance Use/Misuse Prevention
Initiative Grant
This request was discussed at the sunshine meeting. Brandon Halleck approached the Council to
answer any further questions. Randy Gentry expressed his concerns with using opioid settlement
funds and his concerns with the success of this program. Brandon Halleck said the purpose of
grants, like the matching-grant this request is in relation to, is to motivate counties to spend
unused opioid settlement dollars. He said this program uses proven remedies to combat the
impacts of opioid abuse in local communities. Brandon said he was also seeking funding from
multiple sources. Randy Gentry said he would prefer these funds be distributed to fund county
government services. Brandon provided an overview of the programs Chances and Services for
Youth’s provides and the potential effectiveness of them. Steve Ellis expressed his trepidation
with the Council funding a non-profit with county funds. Judge Lakshmi Reddy approached the
Council and expressed support for Brandon’s request. Randy said if the funds were distributed to
fund county services this would allow for oversight he would be more comfortable with. Brenda
Wilson made a motion to approve Additional Appropriation 2025-48. Vicki Weger seconded the
motion. During discussion, Steve Ellis said he would make it a policy of his to require a report of
the finances of any non-profit that requests an appropriation of county funds. David Thompson
