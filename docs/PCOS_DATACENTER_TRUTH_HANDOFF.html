01The opportunity, in three facts

FactWhy it matters
The state says its message is failingIndiana Commerce Secretary Chuck Goodrich, ITIA Summit, 8/27/2026: officials "need to better communicate the benefits of growth." Stated in public, unprompted, by the buyer.
The market is closing anywayRoughly a third of Indiana counties have acted — ~11 with ordinances, 17+ moratoriums, 2 outright bans (Marshall, Cass). Indianapolis paused through 2027.
The information really is brokenThe most-circulated Indiana megawatt figure — 2,400 MW — is Amazon's, from an IURC order that contains zero mentions of LaPorte or Microsoft. Water estimates for the LaPorte site span three orders of magnitude. No MW figure for that site exists in any government record.

The thesis

Companies and agencies do not lack the ability to publish good news about themselves. They lack a venue where skeptics will accept it. Rigor is not a constraint on this business — rigor is the product. Verified good news is the scarce commodity, and nobody is producing it.

03What the evidence already shows

Microsoft made five commitments on the record

At the ITIA Summit (8/27/2026), Microsoft's Director of Infrastructure & Government Affairs enumerated them on stage, tying them to the January 2026 Brad Smith announcement. Speaker confirmed by two independent anchors; 0:14:09–0:15:12.

#CommitmentMeasurable?
1Pay for energy and infrastructure — "residents would not have high electricity bills because of our base and consumption usage"Yes — utility filings
2Minimize water use and "replenish more water than we actually use"Yes — the strongest kind of promise: falsifiable, and it cuts against the dominant local fear
3"We don't want to take a tax abatement" — revenue goes to hospitals, schools, public safetyYes — assessor + city records
4Use workers and labor from the community, including student workersNeeds a defined metric
5Educate on AI infrastructure; invest in local nonprofitsNeeds named amounts

Closing: "So those are five commitments to the community… and we [will] stick to that." None carries a date — which is the first, entirely fair question for right-of-reply, not an accusation.

A verified positive, and a correction worth publishing

Congruent

Mayor Dermody on stage: "we gave 15% direct to our school system" — matches LaPorte's own 3/3/2026 agreement exactly. Microsoft also gave up a 40-year personal-property exemption worth up to $100M in PILOT terms and now pays full property tax. Launch with this.

The correction

The 2,400 MW figure circulating in Indiana is Amazon's. The "538 MW" figure attributed to Microsoft LaPorte appears only on aggregator sites with no cited source. This is probably the single most useful thing the site can tell this community — and it helps Microsoft.

04The moat: six failure modes, found in the field

Each of these produced — or nearly produced — a confident, quotable falsehood about a real person. All are now blocked structurally.

FailureWhat actually happenedBlocked by
Name proximityA threat allegation sat four words from our subject. He did not hold that office on that date.role_tenure
Participant confusionA physical altercation adjacent to him in the record. He was not involved.participant resolution
Unlabeled speaker"I voted against a 0.75 tax" — on his own channel, spoken by his interviewee.diarization + speaker gate
Character voiceHe performed a two-role skit alone. The extracted position was the exact inverse of his real one. Diarization does not catch this.rhetorical-mode gate
Metadata deceitFilename, timestamp and agenda all pointed to the Commerce Secretary. The audio was a legislator.content-confirmed mapping
False fabrication flagWe wrongly accused an AI of inventing a vote and two names — all three turned out real and correctly placed.a fabrication finding is itself a claim

Plus a confirmed one: an AI summarizer produced three different budget figures from a single article. Hence the hard rule — figures are extracted mechanically from the sealed source and never pass through a summarizer.

05Design direction for the site build

What went wrong in my preview — read this first

I built the page theme-responsive (dark + light). CyberHopeAI is dark-committed. On a browser set to light preference it rendered white and looked nothing like the brand. Build dark-only. No light variant, no prefers-color-scheme fallback. The corrected preview is now forced dark and is in the repo as a content reference — treat it as copy and structure, not as visual design.

The signature element

The thermometer. Each commitment is a horizontal gauge that fills as it is independently confirmed. Empty gauges are the feature, not a gap — three of five LaPorte lines legitimately read "no independent measurement exists," and that honesty is the strongest credibility signal on the page. Every figure needs a visible receipt affordance (document + page, or video + timestamp) — that interaction is the product.

06Naming — direction, not a decision

"INformed LaPorte" is rejected — too generic, too Indiana-locked, not data-center-specific. The brief is: data-center focused, works per-project and nationally, tech-credible, not activist.

DirectionExamplesRead
Ground truthGround Truth · GroundTruth DCML term for verified reference data and literally about land. Tech-credible, travels nationally, non-partisan.
The receiptReceipts · Datacenter ReceiptsMatches "show me the receipts." Memorable; possibly too casual for officials.
Infrastructure recordThe Hyperscale Record · Datacenter RecordInstitutional, neutral, obviously about this sector.
MeasurementBaseline · Meridian · The GaugeTies to the thermometer; sounds like instrumentation, not journalism.

Two structural recommendations regardless of the name. First, keep two brands: a public-facing site name, and PrecognitionOS as the engine you license to enterprise — different buyers, and it reinforces the firewall. Second, the name should survive leaving Indiana: the second county is what proves the template travels, and that is the actual business.

07Site scope for the build team

Must ship in V1

Project page · the thermometer · receipt view on every figure · "How we work" · "Why we built this" · "Who we are" with funding disclosure · evidence submission · citizen-question explainers (water, electricity, zoning, jobs, "what if the company leaves")

Explicitly deferred

Comment sections · ratings or scores · company leaderboards · opinion content · statewide map · career/training matching · the "Ask" interface (until the corpus can answer well — a thin Ask makes the whole system look unreliable)

Non-negotiables the site must honor

  • No finding publishes without a logged right-of-reply attempt. Enforced in the database; the front end must respect it.
  • Every number links to its receipt. If there is no source, the number does not appear.
  • "No independent measurement exists" is a first-class display state, not an error or an empty div.
  • Sponsor firewall, stated publicly. Advertisers have no influence over any figure; a funding page names all of them; ad units look visually distinct from evidence.
  • Symmetry. Company-favorable and community-favorable findings both visible at launch.
