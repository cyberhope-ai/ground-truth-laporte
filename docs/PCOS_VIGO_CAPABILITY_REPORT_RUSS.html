Why nobody had done this before

Vigo County publishes its Council minutes as scanned photographs of paper. Every one of the 25 files is unreadable to a computer — you cannot search them, and no search engine has ever indexed a single word inside them. They are public in the legal sense and invisible in the practical sense. PrecognitionOS made all 136 pages fully searchable, recovering 369,000 characters of public record and indexing every statement and every roll-call vote within it. That single step is the difference between a record that exists and a record anyone can actually hold an official to.

The subject

Steve Ellis — owner of Top Guns (Terre Haute, opened 2017), 2026 President of the Vigo County Council, and the Republican nominee for Vigo County Sheriff in the November 2026 general election. His opponent is the incumbent, Sheriff Derek Fell.

This is a demonstration of method, not an endorsement or an attack. Where the record supports Mr. Ellis, this report says so as clearly as where it raises questions. Nothing here has been put to him for response yet — and under our own rules, nothing may be published anywhere until it has been.

Finding 1 · The race is not what it looks like from the outside

Public coverage frames this as a challenger versus an incumbent. The primary record shows something more entangled: both candidates for sheriff are named defendants in the same open federal case over jail conditions — Huerta v. Fell, in the Southern District of Indiana.

Mr. Ellis was substituted into that case on February 3, 2025, upon joining the County Council, and is named in the status report filed August 3, 2026. So is Sheriff Fell. So is every other council member and commissioner.

This must be understood correctly — and most people will get it wrong

These are official-capacity substitutions under Rule 25(d) of the federal rules. When an injunction runs against county offices, whoever holds the office becomes a party automatically. Neither man is personally accused of any misconduct. Saying "the sheriff candidate is a defendant in a federal lawsuit" without that qualifier would be false in substance and legally reckless. We flag it here precisely because a careless system — or a careless opponent — would run exactly that headline.

The accurate reading is more interesting than the careless one: Vigo County's entire governing body, including both men asking to run the sheriff's office, operates under a standing federal injunction and reports to a federal judge to this day. Neither candidate can campaign on the jail as someone else's problem. And the case is not closed — the public database's termination date reflects only the individual damages claims; the class injunction remains in force, with docket entries running continuously through August 2026.

Verified docket entries and party list. Pending the exact date conditions were ruled unconstitutional — public sources give two different dates (September 25 vs. October 10, 2018), so we would pull the court's own record before printing either.

Finding 2 · A documented gap between what he says and how he voted

Mr. Ellis has been strikingly consistent in public over thirteen months on one theme: county spending is out of control. That consistency is itself a finding — he does not change this message between audiences.

"To represent the taxpayer as best as possible, we have to lower spending."WTWO, August 29, 2025
"Every time I turn around, the county is spending 50,000, 100,000, 500,000 dollars on different studies, plans, and drawings… I mean, we waste money like it's water. I've never seen anything like it!"WTWO, November 10, 2025

The Council minutes for October 14, 2025 record the final reading of the Vigo County 2026 budget — $99,199,744. The roll call reads:

"Randy Gentry made a motion to approve the Vigo County 2026 Budget as presented. Steve Ellis seconded the motion. … Randy Gentry — aye; Nancy Allsup — aye; Brenda Wilson — aye; Steve Ellis — aye; Vicki Weger — aye; Brad Anderson — aye; David Thompson — aye. With a vote of 7-0, the motion passed."Vigo County Council Meeting Minutes, 10/14/2025, page 4 — recovered by OCR from a scanned PDF

He did not merely vote for the budget he calls wasteful. He seconded the motion to adopt it.

And here is why we would not print that as hypocrisy

The same set of minutes contains three legitimate defenses, and our system is built to go looking for them before rendering a verdict:

  • The Annual Budget Committee had already cut roughly $1 million from department requests.
  • The increases were attributed to health-insurance costs and a county-wide pay-study adjustment — largely non-discretionary.
  • An Indiana county council is statutorily required to adopt a budget. Voting no is not a costless act of restraint.

So this is logged as Disputed — pending response, not as a contradiction. The correct handling is to place the quote and the vote side by side, ask him to reconcile them, and print his answer in full. If he has a good answer, that is the finding. A system that only produces accusations is not a fact-checker; it is opposition research wearing a lab coat, and readers can tell the difference.

Finding 3 · A specific number, publicly disputed, never resolved

At the same October 14 meeting, during debate over school funding, the minutes record:

"Steve Ellis said he had heard the School Corporation had approximately $150 million, and asked why the School Corporation would spend this money on new facilities instead of repairing and maintaining the old facilities."Council Minutes, 10/14/2025, page 13

He was contradicted in the room, on the record, by the president of the teachers' association, who put the rainy-day fund at $13 million and the cash balance near $35 million. That exchange is a textbook checkable claim: a specific figure, publicly asserted, publicly disputed, and resolvable against state financial filings. Open — this is exactly the kind of item the engine resolves against primary records rather than leaving as he-said/she-said.

Finding 4 · A tax that started three months before the Council intended

The county's 0.25% jail income tax was adopted August 14, 2018, by a 5–2 vote after a five-hour meeting. The Council understood it to take effect January 1, 2019. The state disagreed: under Indiana law an ordinance adopted before September 1 takes effect October 1 of the same year. The Department of Local Government Finance ruled that "Indiana law controls… and does not provide for exceptions."

Taxpayers began paying three months early, and it surfaced only when a resident noticed a smaller paycheck. Verified against the state's own addendum and contemporaneous reporting.

An open legal question we would not print without the court file

Indiana's statute authorizing that jail income tax requires a federal district court order as its predicate. The pre-2003 Vigo jail matter appears to have been resolved by a private settlement agreement in February 2002 — not a consent decree. If that distinction holds, it raises a real question about the legal basis for the tax. We would pull the actual docket before saying a word publicly — this is the kind of claim that must be airtight or unmade.

He is discussing "last night's meeting" — the June 9, 2026 Council meeting whose official minutes we had already recovered independently.

That makes a direct test possible: does what he tells his followers on camera match what the county's own record says happened? Every checkable statement in the video was compared against the minutes.

This is a finding in his favor, and it belongs here as prominently as anything else

On a video made for his own followers, with no interviewer and no obvious pressure to be precise, his account of a public meeting matches the official minutes on every point we could test — including correctly describing a motion he merely voted for rather than claiming credit for making it. A verification system that could not produce this result is not a verification system.

Two items from the same video remain open, and neither is adverse. He states that "one of the local media outlets said 20 jobs — that wasn't correct, it was two," which is a checkable claim about a news outlet's accuracy. And he characterizes the episode as "really wasn't much of a big deal," attributing coverage to "people looking for something to have drama over," while reporting at the time described a residential center facing closure. That is a difference in tone, not a contradiction in fact — and our standard is that emphasis shifts are normal political behavior, labeled as such, never dressed up as dishonesty.

The video trail goes back eight years — and it is about the jail

The same operation runs a YouTube channel whose two earliest videos are from October 2018 — during the fight over the jail tax, six years before he joined the County Council and eight before he ran for sheriff:

  • A 52-minute interview with then-councilman Brendan Kearns (Oct 15, 2018), indexed by its author with these chapter marks: Jail Location · New Jail Plan/Old Jail · Tax Talk · Jail Process/Location · Tax Effect on Community · Convention Center. Kearns was one of only two votes against the 2018 jail tax.
  • A 10-minute interview with the county prosecutor (Oct 18, 2018) about the office and that year's election.

Both channel descriptions are written in the first person — "Steve speaks to current councilman…", "Steve sits down with current prosecutor…" — which is independent supporting evidence of who runs the operation.

Why the 2018 tapes are the most valuable material we have found

They are a record of what he said about the jail and the jail tax as a private citizen, before he held any office — in his own voice, at length, on the exact subject that now defines his race. That enables the strongest possible test: compare the 2018 citizen to the 2026 Council President's votes on jail and corrections funding. Both interviews are captured and transcribed to timestamped text, so any quotation cites the video and the minute mark — the same standard we apply to documents and pages.

Two things the 2018 tape settles immediately. The 52-minute video opens with him saying, on camera: "Hey guys, Steve here from Holding Vigo County Accountable." That moves the question of who runs the page from reported by the newspaper to confirmed in his own recorded voice — and dates the operation to at least October 2018. It also shows a consistent method he states out loud: "if you fight something, you need to be able to provide an alternative solution," and that he ran his own public polling — "when we ran our poll… the majority of people were in favor of building a jail. They just weren't in favor of how it was financed."

The most dangerous thing we found — and it was almost invisible

This is the result we would most want you to see, because it is not a finding about Mr. Ellis at all. It is a finding about what happens when nobody builds the safeguards.

Deep in that 52-minute interview, at the eleven-minute mark, the transcript reads:

"So, as far as the county council, though, I voted against a 0.75 tax for a multitude of reasons, mainly because we can't afford it. What I was good with was a 0.2 tax…"Interview transcript, 0:11:44 — speaker not labeled

It is Steve Ellis's channel. It is Steve Ellis's video. The sentence begins with "I voted." Any ordinary AI tool searching that transcript would report that the Republican nominee for sheriff voted against the jail tax — a clean, quotable, completely false statement about a candidate's record, six weeks before an election.

It is not him. It is the man he is interviewing.

The speaker is Brendan Kearns, the councilman Mr. Ellis invited on. The system established this three separate ways before allowing the quote anywhere near a finding:

  • Chronology, which is decisive. Ellis was elected to the County Council in November 2024. In October 2018 he held no office and could not have voted on anything.
  • A cue inside the recording. The same voice later asks, "how many acres do you have for your top guns facility?" — a question addressed to Ellis. The speaker is the guest.
  • The outside record. Kearns was one of only two votes against that tax, and the same voice says "I want to be a commissioner January 1st" — Kearns was a commissioner candidate that year. Ellis was not.

Consider why this one is worse than the two near-misses earlier in this report. Those needed a name sitting near a damaging phrase. This one needs nothing but the word "I." Transcription produces text with no speakers attached; interview audio is a single track; and every piece of surrounding information — the channel name, the video title, the account that posted it — actively pushes a machine toward the wrong man.

Any system that transcribes video and then searches the words will eventually put a guest's statement in the host's mouth. Applied to political coverage, that is not a bug — it is an unattended machine for destroying reputations. Our rule now: no first-person statement may ever be attributed to a named person unless the speaker has been positively identified. Until each voice in these 2018 tapes is separated and confirmed, the entire recording is quarantined — it is our single most valuable piece of material, and it is not usable yet.

We are telling you about the quote we did not use, and the story we did not run. That is the part that is hard to demonstrate and the only part that ultimately matters.

One limitation stated plainly: the Facebook page's Reels tab and full post history sit behind a login and could not be reached. Only the single public video link could be retrieved. That page is his least-filtered channel and remains the most valuable source we have not examined.

"Voters punished the council over the jail tax"Not supported. Two yes-voters lost, one no-voter lost, three yes-voters won — and the yes-voting council president was the top countywide vote-getter in 2020.

A verification system earns its credibility on findings like these — the ones that take a good story away.

What the machine caught that a person would have missed

These are the results we would most want you to look at, because they are the difference between an AI that finds things and an AI that can be trusted with someone's reputation.

Two near-misses in the documents that would each have been defamatory

1. The November 2025 minutes contain a councilman's written statement that he feared for his safety, citing "the threats that were made against me after the last meeting by the Council president." Mr. Ellis's name appears four words away — he read the statement aloud. But Ellis was not the Council president then. A different man held the office; Ellis became president on January 6, 2026. The system verified who held the role on the date of the document across five separate meetings before attaching anything to anyone.

2. A physical altercation at an October 2025 council meeting — deputies separating two officials, a special prosecutor, no charges — also sits adjacent to Ellis in the record. He was not a participant. His ordinance triggered the underlying dispute, and that is all.

Any ordinary AI tool scoring "documents that mention Steve Ellis" would have produced a false and damaging result twice in the same dataset. Resolving who held which office on which date, and who actually participated, is a required stage in our design — not an optional refinement.

A zero that meant "wrong database," not "clean record"

While checking a separate company's public safety record, the federal motor-carrier database returned zero crashes in 24 months — during a period that contained a documented fatal collision. The reason: that carrier operates intrastate only, largely outside the federal reporting regime. The number was accurate and the conclusion would have been completely false. Our engine now treats an empty result from an out-of-scope database as "not covered," never as "none." An automated system without that rule publishes a clean bill of health for a company that never filed with the agency in the first place.

The AI that lied about numbers — caught by cross-checking

During research, the automated page-reader produced three different budget figures and three different bed counts from the same newspaper article on repeated reads. It fabricated precise-looking digits. Every number in this report was therefore re-derived by direct parsing of the source document, not by asking an AI to read and summarize. This is why the architecture seals the original document first and extracts figures mechanically. It is also the single best reason to distrust any "AI fact-checker" that cannot show you the page it read.

Standards this report holds itself to

  • Nothing here has been put to Mr. Ellis. Under our own rules, no finding about a person may be published until they have been offered a real opportunity to respond, with a deadline. That step has not happened, which is one reason this document is confidential.
  • Verified, pending, and open are labeled — never blurred together.
  • His trade is treated as a neutral fact. A candidate who owns a firearms business is handled exactly as one who owns a pharmacy or a towing company.
  • Symmetry. If we publish on a Republican candidate, the same standard gets applied to his Democratic opponent — and in fact the record already shows a point in Sheriff Fell's favor: he was dismissed from a separate 2026 jail lawsuit for lack of personal involvement, and that belongs in any fair account.
  • Source-interest check. Standard practice requires us to test whether whoever brings us a story has a stake in it. We ran that check on the origin of this one and found no connection — no vote, no testimony, no dispute, no dealings between the subject and the source. We mention it because we would rather tell you we checked than have you learn later that we had.

What this means

Everything above came from records that were already public. No confidential sources, no leaks, no inside knowledge of Vigo County — the system simply read what nobody had the time or tooling to read, remembered all of it at once, and refused to assert anything it could not trace to a page.

That is the product. Not an opinion machine, and not a faster way to generate accusations, but an engine that can say: here is the claim, here is the document it fails or survives, here is the page, and here is the proof that the document said this on the day we read it.

The same engine points at anything with a public record — a county council, a school board, a company's filings against its earnings calls, a candidate's speeches against their votes. Vigo County was chosen because you know it well enough to judge whether the output is real.
