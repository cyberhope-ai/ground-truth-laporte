LaPorte First Site
The Truth About Data Centers

Integrating the INformed Indiana concept with the Veritas engine already under construction — and a sharp recommendation about what to build first, what to defer, and the one structural risk that could discredit the whole thing.
For decision · 2026-08-28

The INformed Indiana plan and the Veritas engine are the same system described at two altitudes. Its "PCOS Trust Plane" — ingestion, provenance, entity resolution, claim extraction, verification, corroboration, governance, publication — is the pipeline we already specced and partly built. There is no architectural conflict to resolve. But it adds one thing we genuinely did not have, and that thing turns out to be the whole product for LaPorte.

1 · What the plan adds: the temporal axis

Our engine answers "is this statement true right now?" That is the wrong question for a data center. Almost every public statement about an infrastructure project is a promise about the future: jobs, tax revenue, water use, megawatts, road improvements, training seats. A promise is not false when it is made. It becomes true or false years later, usually after everyone has stopped paying attention.

So the plan's Claim → Commitment → Outcome loop is not a nice-to-have. It is the missing half of the engine, and I have already built it:

Committed: engine/schema/002_promise_ledger.sql

project · commitment · outcome · submission · verification_record, plus a deterministic commitment_status_for() and a public promise_ledger view. Six decisions in it are load-bearing:

  • project.aliases — data centers are announced under LLC shells and codenames. Without alias history, every promise made under the codename detaches from the operator the moment the real name is revealed. This is how accountability normally evaporates.
  • jobs_permanent vs jobs_construction as separate types. Conflating them is the single most common move in economic-development announcements.
  • tax_abatement_usd is a commitment type. What the public gives up belongs in the ledger beside what it is promised.
  • deadline_stated — a promise with no date cannot be missed, which is frequently the point. Publish the absence of a date as its own finding.
  • outcome.authored_by_promisor with a CHECK constraint: self-reported numbers are recorded but can never move a commitment to "met."
  • verification_record is versioned, never overwritten. 82% confidence today can become 98% in six months as a new version, without rewriting history.

The public surface this produces is the entire pitch, and it fits on one line: 600 jobs promised · 491 independently documented · 81.8% · show me the receipts.

2 · The market signal is explicit — and it came from the state

The demand was stated out loud, by the people who most need it solved

At the ITIA summit on August 27, 2026 in Fishers, Indiana Commerce Secretary Chuck Goodrich said state and industry officials "need to better communicate the benefits of growth as large new projects increasingly face opposition." Meanwhile: dozens of Indiana counties have paused data-center development, passed restrictive zoning, or voted projects down — and Indianapolis has finalized a pause through 2027.

Read those two facts together. The state's own economic-development leadership is publicly saying the message is not landing, while the market keeps closing. That is a validated demand signal for a public-education product, stated by the buyer, in public, unprompted. It is also the clearest possible evidence that more brochures will not fix it — the communication being attempted is not being believed.

Which resolves the credibility question, in a way worth being precise about

The site's value to Microsoft and the state is exactly proportional to its credibility with people who distrust Microsoft and the state

This is the strategic core, and it collapses the apparent tension between "promote the good news" and "be rigorous."

A site that only says favorable things is worth nothing to a company, because nobody who needs persuading will read it. Microsoft does not lack the ability to publish good news about itself; it lacks a venue where skeptics will accept it. Rigor is not a constraint on the business model — rigor is the product being sold. Every verified item that cuts against the project is what makes the verified items that favor it believable. The debunking of a false viral claim about water use is worth more to the company than ten press releases, precisely because we would have published it had it been true.

The one boundary I would still hold: a business/editorial firewall

Have interests. Disclose them. Never let them touch the ledger.

Selling ad slots to Hope Training Academy alongside Ivy Tech and other providers materially changes my earlier concern — an open marketplace of training providers is an advertising channel, not a funnel, and that is a normal, honest way to fund civic media. The failure mode is not having commercial interests; it is having undisclosed interests while implying independence.

So: the standard newspaper firewall, stated publicly. Sponsors and advertisers have no input into any verdict, commitment, or outcome record — ever. A funding page names every sponsor. Ad units are visibly labeled and visually distinct from evidence. And SkillDNA's role here is contributor identity verification, not career placement — that is a genuinely good fit and does not create the conflict I flagged. Write these into the governance layer (L10) as enforced rules, not intentions, and the model is defensible to anyone who asks.

4 · The 11 layers, applied to LaPorte

L0–L1

Hardware & network. Fleet nodes for ingestion, OCR, and transcription; Azure for the public surface. Local GPU handles the heavy media work at no marginal cost — the LaPorte corpus is mostly video and scanned PDFs.

L2–L3

Storage & data. Blob for original evidence (video, audio, PDFs), Postgres for the 22-table graph, pgvector for the congruence and search indexes.

L4

QSurface — the receipts layer. Every artifact, verdict, commitment, and outcome sealed into the hash-linked audit chain. This is what "show me the receipts" resolves to, and it is the one thing no competitor and no news outlet has. When the company disputes a number, we hand them a sealed chain instead of an argument.

L5

Semantic. Entity resolution across the project's alias history; role-by-date for every official; claim extraction; commitment detection — deciding whether a statement is an observation, a projection, or a measurable pledge. That classifier is the highest-value new model work.

L6

Application. The twelve pipeline stages, plus public evidence intake with quarantine-by-default.

L7–L8

Presentation & access. Project page, promise ledger, evidence tab on every number, and "Ask" over the sealed corpus only. Public read; contributor and organization portals gated.

L9

Orchestration — multi-provider verification. No single model produces a final answer alone. Claude and Qwen cross-check; the adversary pass runs on a different model than the drafting pass. For a project where a trillion-dollar company may dispute our findings, "two independent models agreed, here is the reasoning" is a materially stronger position than one model's confidence.

L10

Mothership — governance. Publication gates, the right-to-respond policy, correction policy, retention policy for rejected submissions, and the symmetry rule. This is where the neutrality commitments become enforced rules rather than intentions.

5 · Your recordings — a warning worth more than the tape

Meeting recordings are multi-speaker. Every trap we found in Vigo applies, and one is worse here.

The Vigo pilot produced four ways to generate a confident falsehood, and a room containing a mayor, councillors, company representatives, and public commenters is where all four converge:

  • Unlabeled speakers — the failure that nearly attributed an interviewee's vote to our subject. In a council chamber with a dozen voices this is not an edge case, it is the default. Speaker diarization is a hard prerequisite before any recording enters the ledger.
  • Role-by-date — officials change seats; a promise made by "the council president" must attach to whoever held it that day.
  • Rhetorical mode — officials read constituent letters aloud, quote company statements, and pose hypotheticals. Each of those is someone else's words in their mouth.
  • Numbers — never through a summarizer. A misheard "fourteen" for "forty" in a jobs figure is a correction and a credibility hit.

One additional rule specific to your recordings: if any were made in a setting that was not clearly public, or where participants had a reasonable expectation of privacy, they are not publishable evidence regardless of what they contain. Public meetings and public conferences are fair game; a side conversation is not. Establish that provenance per recording before ingestion, not after.

6 · The Thermometer — the right public metaphor

Same data, opposite emotional register — and the positive framing is the more accurate one

A fundraising thermometer starts at zero and climbs. It is a picture of progress toward completion, not a scoreboard of failures. That reframing is better than "promise ledger" in every way that matters, and it is not spin — for a project under construction, "in progress" is the literally correct status for most commitments. A ledger that reads as an indictment on day one would be inaccurate, not merely unfriendly.

The underlying schema does not change at all — commitment + outcome + commitment_status_for() already produce exactly this. What changes is presentation: a filling gauge per commitment, a project-level completion percentage, and dates showing movement over time. The honest version is also the encouraging version, because most of these promises are on their way to being kept.

Thermometer semantics
ClimbingIndependent measurement shows movement toward the targetthe normal state early in a project

Not yet measurableNo independent measurement exists yethonest and common; says nothing bad about anyone

Note what this does for the company: a thermometer at 78% with sealed receipts is a far stronger public statement than a press release claiming success — because a reader can check it. Verified good news is the scarce commodity here, and we would be the only ones producing it.

7 · Answer the questions people are actually asking

You heard citizens at that conference asking about water, electricity, and zoning. That is the education product, and it is demand-driven rather than guessed. Each becomes a short, evidence-backed explainer with every figure carrying an evidence link:

  • Where does the water come from, how much, and who else uses it? — utility agreements, permits, consumption figures
  • Does this raise my electricity bill? — the single most-asked question statewide; IURC filings, rate structures, who pays for interconnection
  • What did the county actually agree to? — zoning, abatement terms, commitments, in plain language
  • What jobs are real, and when? — construction vs. permanent, with the thermometer beside it
  • What happens if the company leaves? — the question nobody answers, and the one that drives the most fear

Two rules make these trustworthy: every number links to its receipt, and where the honest answer is "no one has published this," we say exactly that. "We don't know yet, and here is who would have to tell us" is more persuasive to a skeptical reader than a confident answer, and it also generates the public-records requests that fill the gap.

V1 is one project page, one thermometer, a handful of citizen-question explainers, and authenticated evidence intake. Small enough to be complete — and completeness is what makes it credible.

Defer, deliberately

The plan's own "do not build these" list is right: no forums, no scores, no leaderboards, no company ratings, no political commentary. I would also defer for V1 the statewide map, the multi-topic Learn section beyond the citizen questions above, career matching, and the Ask interface — Ask especially, until the corpus is large enough to answer well. A thin Ask over a small corpus makes the whole system look unreliable at exactly the moment first impressions form.

Add one thing the plan does not have: a plainly-worded "How we work" page — sources hierarchy, verdict scale, correction policy, right-to-respond, funding, and who we are. For a site whose only product is trust, that page is not boilerplate; it is the product's warranty, and it should ship in V1.

10 · My recommendation

Build LaPorte as a complete, single-project education site with a live thermometer and authenticated citizen evidence intake. Achievable in weeks on the schema that now exists, using the pilot's proven ingestion — and it produces the one thing nobody in this fight currently has: verified good news, with receipts.

Hold the rest of the platform. Not because the vision is wrong — it is right, and the state just said so out loud — but because the second county is the proof that matters commercially, and you only get to attempt it if the first one is unimpeachable. Every additional surface in V1 multiplies the chance of the mistake that costs you that.

Then replicate per project. Dozens of Indiana counties have paused development — each pause is a community that needs exactly this, and a template that travels is the actual business. The public demonstration of PCOS is the marketing, exactly as you framed it: the site does not describe what the technology does, it is the technology doing it, in public, where the Commerce Secretary and Microsoft can both watch it work.
