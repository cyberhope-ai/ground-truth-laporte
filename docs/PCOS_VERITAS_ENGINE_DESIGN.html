PCOS Veritas Engine

A verification engine that produces custody-sealed, source-traceable verdicts — for politics, business, finance, and world events. System design, open-source module selection, PCOS/Q integration, and a staged build plan.
Draft v1 · 2026-08-26 · awaiting approval

We already proved the hard part. In one day, the Vigo County pilot pulled 25 scanned government documents nobody had read, OCR'd 136 pages, and found a county council president who seconded a $99.2M budget he publicly calls wasteful — with a page-level citation. The engine is the industrialization of what that pilot did by hand.

The thesis — what makes this different from every fact-checker in the research

ClaimBuster scores claims. SAFE decomposes and searches. FactReasoner assigns probabilities. All of them answer "is this statement true?" — and all of them forget everything the moment they finish.

PCOS answers two questions nobody else does: "Is this person saying the same thing in every room?" (congruence across channels and time) and "Can you still prove it a year from now?" (custody-sealed evidence). Truth without provenance is just a confident opinion. Our seal chain is the moat — the modules below are commodities we assemble around it.

1 · What the Vigo County pilot proved (requirements, not theory)

Every stage in this design exists because the pilot hit the problem in the real world — not because a paper recommended it.

What happenedRequirement it creates
All 25 county minutes were scanned images; text extraction returned 0 usable characters. OCR at 300dpi yielded 369,000 characters.OCR is core infrastructure, not a fallback. Public records are routinely public-but-unreadable — that gap is precisely why nobody checks them.
A statement about "threats by the Council president" sat 4 words from our subject's name. He wasn't president then — a different man was.Role-by-date resolution. The engine must know who held which office on the document's date before attaching anything to anyone.
A second dramatic incident (a physical altercation) also sat adjacent to the subject and was not about him.Participant resolution. Naive "documents mentioning X" scoring would have produced a defamatory result twice in one dataset.
A grep for EMS returned 13 hits — every one a false positive inside items, systems, problems.Entity resolution with word boundaries. Keyword frequency is not evidence.
The budget contradiction had three legitimate defenses sitting in the same minutes (committee cut $1M; increases were insurance/pay-study driven; adopting a budget is statutorily required).A mitigation-search stage. The engine must actively hunt for the exculpatory context before it renders a verdict — or it manufactures scandals.
The county document center 403s ordinary fetchers; item URLs 302-redirect to the real PDF.A resilient acquisition layer that records both the item URL and the resolved file URL.

Two stages exist in no published framework — and they are the product

Stage 08 · Congruence. Every academic system verifies one claim against the world. Ours verifies a person against themselves, across channels and across time. The verdict scale is deliberately fair: CONGRUENT · EMPHASIS SHIFT (different priorities to different audiences — normal politics, not dishonesty) · INCOMPLETE · INCONGRUENT · CONTRADICTED BY RECORD (the highest-value verdict — requires a document-and-page vote citation) · EVOLVED (changed and said so — a virtue, and the engine must be able to tell it apart from a flip-flop).

Stage 09 · Adversary. Before any verdict publishes, a dedicated pass hunts for the strongest case against our own finding. In the pilot this stage is what turned "hypocrite" into "DISPUTED — pending response," which is both fairer and more defensible. An engine that only finds fault is opposition research wearing a lab coat, and readers can smell it.

3 · Architecture — layers and the modules that fill them

Layer 1 · Acquisition

Apify actors for social/web scraping at scale · MCP servers (glama.ai registry) as live tool-feeds into the reasoning loop · SearXNG (self-hosted, 70+ engines, no API keys, no identity leakage) as the default search substrate · direct government portal fetchers with redirect-following and browser-realistic headers · RSS/broadcast-caption ingestion for real-time claim monitoring.

Layer 2 · Custody & Seal — ours

Adapted directly from the eDNA custody module already running in production: sha256 at capture, blob storage with SAS-scoped retrieval, capture timestamps, and append-only registration. Extended here with archive.today/Wayback snapshotting. Nothing enters analysis unsealed; nothing is destroyable from a web endpoint.

Layer 3 · Render

Tesseract 5 (proven in the pilot, 300dpi grayscale, page-marked output) with PaddleOCR or Surya evaluated for tables and poor scans · faster-whisper for speech (already in our stack from the per-speaker Zoom work) · vision models for chart/image claims. Output is always anchored: document + page or media + timestamp.

Layer 4 · Resolve — ours, and the hardest part

Entity resolution with strict word boundaries and a two-identifier match rule. A temporal role registry: who held which office, on which date, per jurisdiction — built from the same minutes we ingest. This is the layer that prevents defamation, and no off-the-shelf component does it.

Layer 5 · Extract

ClaimBuster claim-spotter methodology for check-worthiness scoring (license caution — see §6) · SAFE-style decomposition into self-contained atomic facts (disambiguate pronouns → atomize) · FActScore-style atomic scoring for long-form output.

Layer 6 · Retrieve

Perplexica/Vane (MIT, self-hostable, SearXNG-bundled, model-agnostic) as the research planner and source engine — the closest fit to what we need and permissively licensed · OpenFactCheck as the pluggable retriever/verifier harness (YAML-configured, treats solvers as plugins) · BM25 + vector hybrid over our own sealed corpus, so past evidence compounds instead of being re-fetched.

Layer 7 · Reason

Evidence graph: claims, evidence, and sources as nodes; supports/contradicts as typed edges; source nodes carry credibility attributes. FactReasoner-style probabilistic aggregation (Markov network over atoms and contexts, NLI-derived entailment/contradiction edges) so conflicting evidence produces a calibrated confidence, not a coin flip. Graph paths are the explanation shown to the reader.

Layer 8 · Q Integration — the moat

QSurface / seal chain — every verdict sealed with its full evidence chain, append-only, independently verifiable · QBRAIN (fleet_brain.py) — cross-agent recall so a claim checked once is known fleet-wide forever · Sanctuary ed25519 region keys — every seal is attributable to an authenticated agent · QDNS — inter-agent routing for distributed verification jobs · VerityQ — the legal-grade presentation layer already built (sealed ledger_figures, case timelines, per-figure provenance) becomes the courtroom-ready front end.

Layer 9 · Models

Qwen family (Apache-2.0 on most weights, strong multilingual, agentic tool-use, VL variants for multimodal claims) as the primary local/self-hosted tier · Claude for the high-stakes reasoning stages (decomposition, adversary pass, final synthesis) where judgment quality dominates cost · small fast models (DistilBERT-class) for real-time check-worthiness triage, exactly as ClaimBuster's production API does.

6 · Risks I need you to see before approving

Licensing — two real commercial constraints

ClaimBuster's claim-spotter is GPL-3.0. Linking it into a proprietary product creates copyleft obligations that could force disclosure of our source. Recommendation: reimplement the methodology (it is published research — check-worthiness scoring on linguistic features) rather than importing the GPL code, or isolate it behind a network boundary. Counsel should confirm before a line of it is used.

Llama 3's license is not open source — it carries a large-user threshold and naming/attribution conditions. Recommendation: default to Qwen (Apache-2.0 on most weights) and Claude, and avoid building anything load-bearing on Llama licensing. Verify the specific weight's license at adoption time; they differ model to model.

Legal exposure is the real risk, not technical failure

We are publishing findings about named living people. The pilot produced two near-miss defamatory attributions in a single dataset. Mandatory before any public launch: media-liability insurance, a standing relationship with a media-law attorney, a written pre-publication review gate, and the right-of-reply stage enforced in code — a verdict without a logged reply attempt should be structurally unpublishable.

The source research needs fact-checking — and that is the lesson

The Gemini report contains an internal contradiction: one pass describes DeepMind's SAFE in technical detail; a later pass declares "there is no evidence of a tool or research project with this name from Google DeepMind." (SAFE is real — the DeepMind/Stanford long-form factuality work.) The report also asserts a "Vane (formerly Perplexica), rebranded March 2026" and cites Qwen versions beyond my verified knowledge — all flagged as unverified pending a primary-source check of each repository and license. A research document about automated fact-checking that fails its own standard is the single best argument for building this engine. Every module in §3 gets verified at its source repo before adoption.

Two things to decide before P0

1 · Neutrality posture. A verification engine's only asset is being trusted by people who dislike its findings. If our first published subject is a Republican sheriff candidate, the second should be a Democrat — and the standard applied must be visibly identical. Symmetry is a product requirement, not an ethic we bolt on later.

2 · Source disclosure policy. The Ellis story came from a tip. We should adopt now: always disclose that a story originated from a tip, and always state whether the tipster has an interest we checked. Doing this from article one costs nothing; retrofitting it after someone else discovers it costs everything.

7 · Realtime deployment — yes, but on three clocks

Short answer: yes. But "realtime fact-checking" is two different products wearing the same name, and conflating them is how this platform would get sued.

The rule that governs the whole realtime design

Realtime is safe for RECALL. Realtime is dangerous for JUDGMENT.
Instantly telling a viewer "this exact claim was verified three months ago, here is the sealed record" is safe — the adjudication already happened, survived the adversary pass, and carries its evidence chain. Instantly declaring a brand-new claim FALSE on air is a defamation engine with a subscription model. The architecture below makes that distinction structural rather than a matter of operator discipline.

The three clocks

TierLatencyWhat it doesWhat it may publish
T1 · REFLEX< 1 secIngest live text (broadcast captions, streams, social firehose, council audio via ASR). Score check-worthiness. Match against the sealed verdict corpus and the subject's congruence index.Prior sealed verdicts only — "previously verified," "he said the opposite on 11/10/25, sealed record #…". Never a new judgment.
T2 · RAPIDseconds – minutesNovel claim: decompose to atomic facts, retrieve evidence (SearXNG/Perplexica + MCP live feeds + our sealed corpus), evidence graph, probabilistic verdict with confidence.Provisional, labeled — shown to editors and internal dashboards. Public only as "unverified claim detected," never as a verdict.
T3 · DELIBERATEhours – daysMitigation/adversary pass, primary-record retrieval (dockets, PDFs, FOIA/APRA), role-and-participant resolution, human editorial review, right of reply.Publishable verdict, sealed with full evidence chain. This is the only tier that produces public findings about people.

Why T1 gets faster and cheaper the longer we run — the compounding asset

A T3 verdict, once sealed, becomes a T1 lookup forever. Political claims are repeated relentlessly — the same budget figure, the same crime statistic, the same biography line, across a whole campaign. After one cycle of a race, most of what a candidate says on a debate stage is a restatement of something already adjudicated and sealed. That is when realtime stops being a demo and becomes genuinely useful: not "the AI thinks this is false," but "this claim has been checked; here is the primary document, and here is what he said differently in March." QBRAIN makes that recall fleet-wide and permanent. Competitors re-fetch the world on every query; we answer from memory that is signed and auditable.

Realtime congruence — the feature nobody else can ship

Congruence is where realtime gets genuinely powerful, because it is a lookup, not a search. If a subject's statement history is already extracted, sealed, and indexed by issue, then testing a live sentence against everything they have ever said on that issue is a vector query against a pre-built index — milliseconds, not minutes. That is the on-air moment: a candidate makes a claim, and within a second the operator sees the three occasions they said something materially different, each with a sealed citation. No fact-checking framework in the surveyed research does this at all, at any latency.

What realtime unlocks commercially

  • Live debate / town-hall companion — the flagship demo, and the one that sells: sealed prior verdicts and congruence hits surfacing as a candidate speaks.
  • Newsroom console — reporters get instant recall of what a subject has said before, with citations, instead of re-reading a year of coverage.
  • Meeting monitor — point it at any public body's stream; it flags claims, matches them to prior votes, and produces a sealed transcript record. The Vigo pilot is exactly this, run manually.
  • Earnings-call / disclosure congruence — same engine, business vertical: what the CEO says on the call versus what the filing says. High-value, and the subjects can afford the product.

Honest constraints

ASR sets the floor. Sub-second end-to-end on live broadcast is not realistic; ~1–3 seconds is. Fine for an operator console, wrong for an on-screen chyron that must keep pace with speech.

Novel claims cannot be adjudicated at speed — and we should never pretend otherwise. The product promise must be "instant recall plus flagged uncertainty," not "instant truth." Any competitor promising the latter is either lying or about to be sued; our differentiator is being the one that doesn't.

Numbers never pass through a summarizer. Field-proven in the pilot: repeated fetches of the same article produced three different budget figures and three different bed counts. At realtime volume that failure mode scales into systematic fabrication. Figures are extracted by deterministic parsing of the sealed artifact, with the page anchor retained — no exceptions, at any tier.

Where realtime fits the build plan

It is P2 — and it should stay there. T1 is only valuable in proportion to the size of the sealed verdict corpus behind it; shipping realtime before P1 produces a fast system with nothing to remember, which is a demo that impresses once and then embarrasses. Build the corpus and the seal chain first, then turn on the clock. The realtime layer is roughly six weeks of work on top of P1 — and worth an order of magnitude more with a year of sealed verdicts behind it than with none.

8 · Recommendation

Approve P0 and P1. They are cheap, they are built almost entirely from work already done, and they produce two things of immediate value: a publishable article that demonstrates the method, and a roll-call vote parser that makes every county government in Indiana queryable — which is a defensible data asset regardless of where the news product goes.

Hold P2–P4 until P1 ships, for a reason worth stating plainly: the live-data plane is the fun part and the least differentiating. Scrapers and MCP feeds are commodities. The custody chain, the congruence matrix, and the resolution layer are what nobody else has — and they are what makes a verdict survive contact with a lawyer.
